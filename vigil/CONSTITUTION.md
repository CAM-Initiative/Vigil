# VIGIL Observatory BETA Constitution

**Status:** BETA — draft, not yet adopted  
**Version:** 0.1.0-beta  
**Authority level:** Constitutional governance layer

## Preamble

VIGIL Observatory preserves public evidence of AI-related occurrences and performs bounded, reviewable governance adjudication over that evidence.

VIGIL does not determine liability, create legal findings, establish final factual truth or claim infallibility. Its legitimacy depends on whether its conclusions are produced, challenged, corrected and preserved through a transparent and reproducible process.

This Constitution is intentionally **BETA**. It does not freeze the present taxonomy, adjudication system or analytical results. It governs how analytical authority is exercised while those systems mature.

> **No adjudication acquires legitimacy merely because it exists in the corpus. Its authority derives from the quality, traceability and governed status of the process that produced it.**

## Article 1 — Constitutional scope

This Constitution governs who may propose, perform, review, approve and implement changes to VIGIL's canonical analytical state; how incomplete, contested, corrected and superseded adjudications are treated; how challenges are received and reviewed; how taxonomy insufficiency is handled; and how this Constitution may be amended.

Operational procedure is delegated to subordinate instruments including `vigil/MAINTAINERS.md`, `vigil/AGENTS.md`, `vigil/docs/maintenance/INCIDENT-ADJUDICATION-WORKFLOW.md`, schemas, the adjudication matrix, manifests and validators.

Where a subordinate rule conflicts with this Constitution, the conflict must be escalated for human review.

## Article 2 — Foundational principles

### 2.1 Evidence before conclusion

VIGIL conclusions must be bounded by preserved evidence, but VIGIL is not bound by the interpretation supplied by a publisher, developer, affected party, regulator, journalist, researcher or other source.

VIGIL may accept and rely on evidence supplied by a source while reaching a different analytical conclusion about what that evidence establishes across factual record, taxonomy, Harm Impact and governance interpretation.

Source characterisations such as "governance failure", "safety failure", "successful safeguard", "misuse", "harm", "benign outcome" or similar evaluative labels are therefore evidence about the source's position, not the VIGIL verdict.

Materialised adverse outcomes, public controversy, reputational effects, humiliation, exclusion, dignity impacts and other evidenced consequences may directly support Harm Impact findings where they satisfy the applicable VIGIL-HIM criteria. Their existence does not, however, by itself establish a particular taxonomy mechanism or governance diagnosis.

### 2.2 Separate adjudications

Factual, taxonomy, harm and governance adjudications are distinct analytical acts. A conclusion in one domain is not automatic proof in another.

### 2.3 Incompleteness is valid

VIGIL must be able to say **adjudication incomplete**. No agent, maintainer, schema requirement or publication process may manufacture a classification because an analytical field is empty or because a completed answer would be convenient.

### 2.4 Canonical does not mean infallible

A canonical adjudication is the current governed VIGIL result. It is not final truth, permanent truth, a legal determination or immunity from challenge.

### 2.5 Correction is ordinary governance

Re-adjudication is legitimate when justified by new evidence, factual correction, taxonomy change, taxonomy gap, clause-level analysis, comparator inconsistency, methodological defect or other documented grounds. Prior error does not authorise silent replacement.

### 2.6 No forced taxonomy fit

An Incident must not be forced into the nearest available Fidelity Class. If no class faithfully represents an evidenced mechanism, VIGIL may record no mapping, unresolved recognition facts, an ambiguous boundary or a taxonomy action requirement.

### 2.7 Authority follows process

Repository write access, maintainer status, authorship, model confidence, contributor status or founder status do not by themselves confer epistemic authority.

## Article 3 — Analytical authority during BETA

During BETA, substantive taxonomy boundaries, taxonomy admission, governance interpretation, contested adjudications and constitutional amendment remain under human governance authority exercised by CAM Initiative as VIGIL's steward.

That authority is not a licence for arbitrary analytical control. It must operate through documented VIGIL procedure and may not be used to silently rewrite evidence, suppress a valid challenge or bypass required re-adjudication.

AI systems may gather evidence, perform structured clause-level analysis, test recognition criteria and exclusions, compare Incidents, prepare adjudication matrices and manifests, identify taxonomy gaps, recommend dispositions and draft canonical changes. AI systems may not treat their own prior output or confidence as authority for a contested conclusion.

Maintainers may execute governed changes. Technical ability to merge a change does not create independent authority to decide a contested analytical question.

Future constitutional versions may delegate authority to independent reviewers, panels, domain stewards or community institutions, but any delegation must define scope, conflicts, procedure, provenance and review rights.

## Article 4 — Adjudication states

For constitutional purposes VIGIL recognises:

- **Adjudication incomplete** — required work has not been completed under the current workflow. Partial findings must not be promoted into a whole-Incident conclusion.
- **Canonical** — the current governed result produced through the applicable procedure.
- **Under challenge** — a sufficiently specified challenge has been accepted for review. The existing result remains visible while review proceeds.
- **Superseded** — a prior adjudication has been replaced through governed re-adjudication and remains reconstructable through provenance.
- **Transitional** — legacy analytical content exists but has not completed the present adjudication standard. It may be preserved without being granted stronger authority merely because it is old.

## Article 5 — Minimum conditions for authoritative adjudication

A populated field is not enough. Where required by the current workflow, a completed adjudication requires a bounded factual record, current taxonomy, clause-level analysis where materially applicable, testing of recognition conditions and exclusions, explicit disposition of prior mappings, separate treatment of taxonomy roles and harm, treatment of material uncertainty, comparator review where relevant, and sufficient validation and provenance to reconstruct the decision.

Failure to complete a required element must produce an incomplete or qualified state rather than fabricated analytical closure.

## Article 6 — Standing to challenge

Any person or entity may question or challenge a VIGIL adjudication, including affected persons, researchers, developers, providers, regulators, civil-society organisations, academics, contributors, maintainers and members of the public.

Standing to challenge does not confer authority to determine the outcome.

A formal challenge should identify, so far as reasonably possible, the Incident or analytical object, the finding in dispute, the asserted ground, supporting evidence or argument, and the correction or question sought. A challenge must not be rejected merely because the challenger lacks VIGIL terminology if the substantive issue can be identified.

General disagreement may be recorded without automatically reopening the record. Re-adjudication is required when a challenge identifies a material evidentiary, analytical, taxonomy, consistency, methodological or constitutional issue capable of affecting the governed result.

## Article 7 — Grounds for re-adjudication

Grounds include new evidence; factual or source error; taxonomy misapplication; taxonomy development; taxonomy gap; clause-level defect; comparator inconsistency; methodological defect; constitutional defect; or another documented analytical defect capable of changing the result.

Re-adjudication must not be used merely to seek a preferred outcome after a procedurally sound result.

## Article 8 — Challenge procedure

A challenge attaches to the existing adjudication; it does not overwrite it.

The baseline decision, evidence state, taxonomy version and relevant provenance must be preserved. The challenged question must be rerun from the governing evidence, source clauses, taxonomy definitions, recognition criteria and exclusions. The reviewer must not defend the existing VIGIL answer by default.

Where new evidence is introduced, the review should distinguish what could have been established on the original record from what changes only because of later evidence.

Where inconsistent treatment is alleged, relevant comparator Incidents must be reviewed. Inconsistency is a reason to examine the threshold, not to choose whichever prior decision is more convenient.

A challenge may be **affirmed**, **amended**, **reversed**, **reclassified as incomplete**, **unresolved**, treated as an **ambiguous boundary**, escalated as **taxonomy action required**, or returned for **procedural remediation**.

No substantive reversal may occur silently. The record must preserve what changed, why, the controlling evidence and taxonomy version, the reviewer, and the human authority required during BETA.

## Article 9 — Taxonomy integrity

The taxonomy itself is governable. Repeated inability to represent a materially evidenced mechanism may reveal a taxonomy defect rather than an Incident defect.

Agents and maintainers must not repair a taxonomy gap by informally broadening a Fidelity Class inside an Incident. Changes to class scope, invariant, recognition criteria or exclusions must occur through the governed taxonomy amendment process.

VIGIL is not failure-only. A materially engaged invariant may be recorded as successfully preserved, and an occurrence may illuminate an ambiguous boundary. Mere absence of failure is not proof of a successful invariant.

## Article 10 — Provenance and institutional memory

VIGIL must preserve enough history to explain material changes in analytical position: what it previously concluded, under what evidence and taxonomy, what triggered reconsideration, what the later adjudication concluded and why.

Git history forms part of this architecture but does not replace required structured provenance.

VIGIL must not rewrite its institutional history to create the appearance that the current answer was always the answer.

## Article 11 — Conflicts and affected parties

An affected party may challenge an adjudication and provide evidence, technical information or argument. Participation does not grant veto authority, and an interested source is not automatically excluded merely because it has an interest in the outcome.

During BETA, material reviewer conflicts should be disclosed and, where practicable, independent or additional review sought. Future versions should formalise recusal rules as participation expands.

## Article 12 — Publication integrity

Public presentation must not imply greater certainty or procedural completeness than the governed state supports. Incomplete adjudication must not be presented as completed classification; partial findings must not be promoted into whole-Incident conclusions; superseded adjudications must not be presented as current; accepted challenges should be qualified where materially relevant; and technical schema validity must not be mistaken for substantive adjudication.

## Article 13 — Constitutional amendment

Because this Constitution is BETA, amendment is expected. Amendment must nevertheless be deliberate, versioned and reviewable.

A constitutional amendment must identify the provision changed, the reason, practical governance effect, subordinate rules requiring corresponding amendment, any change in authority, and the human approval by which the new version becomes operative during BETA.

A validator, schema, agent instruction, maintainer convention or one-off adjudication may not silently amend this Constitution.

## Article 14 — BETA transition rule

Adoption of this Constitution does not certify every existing VIGIL adjudication as compliant with the current standard.

Legacy and partially rebuilt records may remain while systematic re-adjudication proceeds. Where current controls determine that a record has not completed present-day adjudication, it must be treated or publicly represented as incomplete to the extent required by the publication contract.

Historical classifications remain provenance. They are not constitutionally insulated from correction.

This rule allows VIGIL to improve without pretending earlier methods were stronger than they were and without erasing the history through which the current methodology developed.

## Article 15 — Constitutional interpretation

This Constitution should be interpreted in favour of evidentiary fidelity, explicit uncertainty, reproducible adjudication, non-destructive correction, separation of analytical domains, challenge without capture, authority bounded by process, preservation of institutional memory and VIGIL's ability to change its mind for documented reasons.

Where two interpretations remain plausible during BETA, prefer the interpretation that avoids manufacturing certainty or silently expanding analytical authority.

## Governance hierarchy

1. VIGIL Observatory BETA Constitution
2. VIGIL Maintainer Guide and Agent Instructions
3. Incident Adjudication Workflow and taxonomy amendment procedures
4. Schemas, adjudication matrix, manifests and validators
5. Canonical Incident and taxonomy state
6. Generated public projections

Subordinate controls operationalise the Constitution. They do not independently redefine its allocation of authority.
