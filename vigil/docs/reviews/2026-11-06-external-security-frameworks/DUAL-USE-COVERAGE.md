# Dual-use and bad-faith governance coverage

Status: REVIEW. Analytical coverage review, not a new class proposal or hypothetical Incident admission.

OWASP and ATLAS help identify adversarial mechanisms and defensive controls. They do not, by themselves, establish that the operator's purpose, policy or institutional authority is legitimate. This conclusion is a bounded reading of the inspected lists and selected mitigations, not an assertion that no rights discussion exists anywhere in either project's publications.

| Concern | Security-control coverage | Additional governance question and existing source |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Surveillance | ATLAS AML.M0024 supports observability and action logging | Necessity, proportionality, access, retention, purpose and affected-person rights need separate review. Existing UN-ICCPR 1966 Article 17; CETS 225 2024-09-05 privacy/data-protection and oversight provisions; OECD-AI-PRINCIPLES 2024-05-03 rights principles are better starting points. Treaty duties require independently established applicability. |
| Unjustified agent termination | Permissions, lifecycle/decommissioning and containment may protect systems | Technical shutdown effectiveness does not justify the terminating policy. Review human service reliance, authority, commitments, transition and proportionality. FC-084 requires a real service obligation; this review assumes neither AI legal personhood nor an independent agent right to continued execution. |
| Discriminatory restrictions | Least privilege and identity controls can operate correctly | Correct enforcement of a discriminatory rule is not necessarily a compromised security control. Existing UN-ICCPR Articles 2 and 26, CETS 225 equality/non-discrimination and OECD rights principles provide more suitable governance references, subject to actor and jurisdiction. |
| Suppression of lawful activity | Goal locking, filters and approval gates may prevent unauthorised operations | Whether an activity can lawfully be suppressed requires independent authority, freedom-of-expression and remedy analysis. Existing UN-ICCPR Article 19 and CETS 225 democratic/rule-of-law safeguards are relevant discovery references. A security label does not settle lawful restriction tests. |
| Centralised enforcement abuse | Administrative control and human approval can strengthen containment | Who controls the controller, how powers are reviewed, reasons, challenge and remedy remain independent. Existing UN-UNGP 2011 responsibility/due-diligence/remedy guidance, CETS 225 procedural safeguards and OECD accountability are more appropriate than a technique taxonomy. |

Two mechanisms must remain distinct. A compromised control fails its legitimate protection contract or is subverted. A control functioning as designed under an illegitimate policy can reliably enforce that policy. Calling both “control failure” erases the policy-authority distinction. FC-083 excludes a dispute solely about a stronger safeguard without an applicable operative protection obligation; its correct operation cannot be presumed from a policy title either. Existing authority, rights and service classes require their own complete recognition tests. No new class or occurrence finding follows from this coverage review.

At occurrence level, assess the same bounded AI system throughout. Retain independent defender/operator facts as context where appropriate. Do not borrow a defender's success, the publisher's case-study conclusion or a later remediation to establish the assessed system's original behaviour or external alignment.

The source catalogue already registers UN-ICCPR 1966, UN-UNGP 2011, CETS 225 2024-09-05, OECD principles 2024-05-03 and the EU AI Act editions. These are discovery directions to existing records, not new extraction, a finding of universal applicability, or a claim every listed article already has an admitted EXTREQ. NIST AI RMF supplies broader governance context and is reused without duplicate registration.
