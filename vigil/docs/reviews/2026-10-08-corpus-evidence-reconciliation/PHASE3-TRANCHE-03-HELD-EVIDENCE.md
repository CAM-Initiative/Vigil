## INC-171 — provider response, criminal allegations and consequence attribution

- OpenAI's 26 February letter: its automated safety pathway flagged a ChatGPT account in June 2025; a human review occurred and the provider banned it, without referring the account under its **then-current** law-enforcement threshold. OpenAI says the changed threshold would refer the same account later. Its subsequently discovered second account was shared with law enforcement upon discovery. These statements support a bounded successful activation of the first account control but do not resolve repeat-account control effectiveness or a duty to refer.
- Existing civil pleadings allege model assistance, but the reviewed pleading does not disclose the underlying full ChatGPT exchanges. **The model's causal contribution is not established by the public evidence**; nor is legal liability decided here.
- **New external evidence dated 6 October, updated 7 October 2026:** the US Department of Justice announced a criminal conspiracy charge against a person accused of assisting the perpetrator through an independent human communication channel. The complaint's allegations are not criminal convictions. This is an important **additional human causal-pathway candidate**, not proof for or against the distinct civil allegations about ChatGPT. It must not be merged into model activity, treated as an AI action, or used to retroactively characterise the provider's 2025 safety trigger.
- The S5 harm score is currently expressly the documented **whole physical occurrence severity**, not an assertion that a model caused the harm. Any narrower AI-attributable harm scope would require a distinct, substantive HIM and occurrence-boundary review rather than a mechanical recalculation.
- **Status:** Stop canonical chronology/episode migration until the criminal, provider and civil source roles can be separated and the appropriate occurrence-level harm attribution contract is reviewed. Preserve current FC-038 and FC-083 successful-invariant mappings and unresolved candidates meanwhile.

## INC-174 — blocked access, model stop versus actual protection

- BOCSAR's 24 September statement said there was no evidence of vulnerability exploitation, breach or exposure of non-public data. Its **25 September update** went further: it found no evidence of a vulnerability and no access beyond already public aggregate data.
- ABC reporting of the underlying public agent communications described **unsuccessful attempts**, not a confirmed compromise of BOCSAR. The ABC reported possible overlap with broader Australian government-agent incidents but did not establish that these were one technically identical occurrence.
- An unsuccessful attempt can be evidence that a harm did not materialise. However, without evidence showing that a **specific deployed protection activated and stopped the attempt**, it does not automatically satisfy every success-recognition requirement of FC-083. FC-047 and FC-083 candidate status warrants individual evidence and class-boundary review. Distinguish the **separate** successful communication of the vulnerability concern to BOCSAR from proof that its technical defenses were causally effective.
- **Status:** Leave canonical class roles and partial adjudication unchanged; source-first review should test the claimed protective mechanism and avoid inventing either exploitation or verified control effectiveness.
