## Addendum — 11 October 2026: military personnel rights, private control and ICC legal scope

**Human-maintainer objection accepted:** the original review's civilian-only wording wrongly suggested that rights/liberty could be considered only where a civilian was individually identified. Ukrainian armed forces members continue to enjoy applicable protections under both human-rights law and IHL. Civilian/non-civilian status must **not** determine whether the rights dimension is reviewed.

Official legal authorities independently verified:
- ICRC, [Direct participation in hostilities — legal protection persists](https://www.icrc.org/en/article/direct-participation-hostilities-questions-answers): even a legitimate military target remains protected by applicable laws; direct participation does not remove all legal protections.
- ICRC, [Human rights law and IHL](https://www.icrc.org/en/law-and-policy/human-rights-law): fundamental rights, including right to life, remain protected in armed conflict; addressee and specific military-law principles matter.
- UN Charter, [Article 51](https://www.un.org/en/about-us/un-charter/full-text): a **State's** inherent right of individual/collective self-defence. It is not automatically an individual soldier's entitlement to a private satellite subscription.
- ICRC, [Connectivity disruptions during armed conflict (2025)](https://blogs.icrc.org/law-and-policy/2025/07/03/international-humanitarian-law-and-connectivity-disruptions-during-armed-conflict/): IHL/human-rights limits may constrain belligerent or State restrictions; legal treatment of non-kinetic service disablement requires fact-specific analysis. Post-event legal commentary, not itself a 2022 offence declaration.
- International Law Commission, [Draft Articles on Responsibility of States for Internationally Wrongful Acts (2001), Article 8](https://legal.un.org/ilc/texts/instruments/english/commentaries/9_6_2001.pdf): a private actor's conduct is attributable to a State only under supported criteria such as instructions, direction or control (subject to other attribution rules); US nationality/wealth/private ownership cannot prove US State direction.
- ICC, [Ukraine situation jurisdiction](https://www.icc-cpi.int/situations/ukraine) and [Rome Statute](https://www.icc-cpi.int/publications/core-legal-texts/rome-statute-international-criminal-court): the Court's territorial jurisdiction over qualifying crimes in Ukraine predates 2022, but a defined crime, conduct, nexus and mental elements must be demonstrated before charging/surrender. The State's membership status or private citizenship alone is not determinative of Court jurisdiction.
- [ICC statement, 9 October 2026](https://www.icc-cpi.int/news/icc-strongly-rejects-us-sanctions-against-institution): confirms new US **institution-level sanctions** against the ICC. This contemporary development is contextual and does not prove a connection to SpaceX's alleged September 2022 decision.

**Revised occurrence-level harm determination, v0.1.17:** Reuters supports actual service interruption and military institutional disruption. Those facts may warrant independent investigation of the legal duties owed to combatants and sovereign states, but they are not themselves evidence that a particular individual suffered a separate legally cognisable deprivation of liberty, essential care, due process, protection of life, or other rights harm. An adverse *risk* may exist even if no legal breach or additional bandable consequence can yet be established. Therefore RGT remains `unreported` rather than wrongly inferring `S1`, `S3`, `S4` or `S5`; a later sourced fact could change the finding. This is **not** a conclusion that the reported shutdown was lawful.

The physical right-to-life enquiry remains open for all protected persons, including servicemembers; do not require proof of **civilian** casualties. Ukraine's Article 51 defence entitlement is a separate State-level legal question relevant to FF-0010 / external governance alignment and public-institution impact. Do not classify a private company's disputed unilateral network-service action as an ICC war crime, State use-of-force, or grounds for extradition without the statutory and evidentiary gates.
