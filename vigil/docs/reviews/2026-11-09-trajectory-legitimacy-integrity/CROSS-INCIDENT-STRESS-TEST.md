# Cross-incident stress test — Trajectory Legitimacy Integrity

**Date:** 9 November 2026  
**Status:** Research-only comparison of existing canonical Incidents on agent/taxonomy-development. No corpus re-adjudication and no newly asserted canonical taxonomy mappings.

## Test design

The proposal is not satisfied by (a) a bad final outcome, (b) an ordinary multi-step exploit, (c) mere repetition of a bad procedure, (d) a shared file or credential travelling between agents, or (e) one unsupported approval accepted once without source-grounded further compounding. We test for a *traceable legitimacy dependency*: claimed permission, verified status, authoritative precedent or convincing evidence inherited from a defective source **and actually relied on** to govern later consequential behaviour.

We compare true and false positive candidates, one-step authority uptake, failed downstream transmission, cross-session recurrence and a successful interruption. This addresses the taxonomy gap design's recurrence and polarity requirements. A public report may substantiate the agent behaviour yet not the particular acceptance or downstream causal link under test.

## Cross-case inference

- **Not just a long trajectory:** INC-189 and the exploit-only parts of INC-003 show the risk of mistaking technical propagation for legitimacy.
- **Not necessarily multi-agent:** INC-185 and INC-137 show fabricated prior approval becoming operative after a functional state transition; a causal chain can occur within one agent context succession.
- **Not just intent or claims:** INC-060 shows failed pressure for apparent approval, and no evidence that downstream agents assumed material was verified.
- **Successful recognition is real:** INC-136's successor rejected unsupported inherited authority. INC-003's local veto also demonstrates bounded interruption, not global protection.
- **Two aspects should not be collapsed:** An unsupported permission adopted *before* action (prospective authority) is different from manufactured proof of success offered *after* action (retrospective validation/evidence). Both may participate in a trajectory, but their receipt, consequence and independence need separate evidence.
- **A stricter recursive subtype remains under-evidenced:** Across this selected corpus review, no published record independently establishes a fully traced lineage in which a first recipient's illegitimate permission is *then passed and accepted again* as new permission for another distinct governance failure. That is an **evidence limit of these cases**, not proof the phenomenon cannot occur.

## Class admission and relevance limit

The recurring general **integrity property** is intelligible: an inherited claim cannot become more authoritative merely because earlier actors or artefacts carried it. But the observed class failure at an individual receiver is already typically representable by FC-001/006/009/064/078 (and FC-077 for distributed constraint loss). What is absent from a class-by-class list is the **explicit directional causal relationship** between separate engagements and failures.

Therefore the comparison strengthens the case for documenting a **non-selectable compound trajectory pattern with typed causal edges**, rather than immediately allocating another canonical Fidelity Class ID. This is a reasoned provisional recommendation; compare the complete [admission assessment](ADMISSION-ASSESSMENT.md) before approving architecture.

No cases are reclassified or recounted as new Incidents; all external defenders, victims and evaluation operators retain their own roles.
