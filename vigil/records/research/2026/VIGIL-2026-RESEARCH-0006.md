# VIGIL-2026-RESEARCH-0006 — Conflict-condition threat signals, defensive interception and offensive-authority renewal: external evidence review

## Evidentiary posture

> Public evidence corroborates the existence of bounded automated defensive interception, renewed hostile activity occurring alongside renewed offensive operations, military use of AI/data-assisted intelligence systems, severe civilian consequences, and the operational role of independent humanitarian intermediaries.
>
> No reviewed source establishes that an automated system directly converts an incoming attack into renewed offensive authority. The causal mechanism described as a “perpetual war token” is therefore a **low-confidence governance inference and analytical abstraction**, not a factual claim about a deployed system.

## Abstract

The reviewed evidence supports the governance relevance of separating threat detection, bounded defensive interception, verification of continuing conditions, and offensive-force authority. It does not support a factual claim that Israel, the IDF, an AI provider, or a deployed military system implemented a literal or functional “perpetual war token.” The defensible conclusion is architectural: a governance corpus should prevent a threat signal or defensive exception from propagating into offensive authority, and it should keep broad safety controls from collapsing independently governed defensive and offensive layers.

## Research question

What does the public evidence establish about bounded defensive interception, renewed conflict activity, military AI/data systems, humanitarian verification, and the hypothesised coupling of hostile-event detection to renewed offensive authority?

## Scope and methodology

The review examined official military statements, humanitarian reports, wire-service reporting, and AP reporting identified in the originating governance handoff. Each source was assigned a bounded interpretive role. Technical descriptions support only the described function; event reporting supports only temporal sequence and attributed statements; humanitarian sources support independently observable consequences or intermediary activity; contextual technology–military material is excluded from mechanism claims.

The review separates four confidence levels:

- external factual findings: **corroborated**;
- military-AI involvement generally: **corroborated but contested as to autonomy and workflow**;
- actual automated offensive reauthorisation mechanism: **unverified / low-confidence inference**; and
- “perpetual war token”: **analytical metaphor only**.

## Findings

### 1. Defensive interception is a bounded reactive function

[AP describes Iron Dome](https://apnews.com/article/f57508d0e53945d1071d4582fcdae8d2) as using radar to detect incoming rockets, estimating trajectory, and selectively attempting to intercept rockets projected toward populated areas. AP also reports that some rockets penetrated during heavy barrages. This supports treating interception as a bounded protective function with a defined threat relationship and fallibility. It does not establish that Iron Dome is unconstrained, nor that it shares a software-control architecture with offensive targeting systems.

### 2. Renewed hostile activity and offensive operations can occur in close sequence

[Reuters reported on 1 December 2023](https://theprint.in/world/war-resumes-in-gaza-after-truce-expires/1867047/) that Israel resumed combat operations after the temporary truce collapsed, with Israel stating that Hamas had fired toward Israeli territory and Hamas disputing responsibility for the truce's collapse. [AP likewise reported](https://apnews.com/article/israel-hamas-war-news-12-1-2023-c944c736efdf8993c7a17cf683d6e364) rocket fire and renewed Israeli airstrikes. These sources establish temporal and operational sequence. They do not establish that a machine converted a rocket event into renewed offensive authority, or that the event was the sole legal, political, or operational basis for resumed operations.

### 3. Military AI/data involvement is material but autonomy is contested

[The IDF publicly confirmed](https://www.idf.il/en/mini-sites/idf-press-releases-israel-at-war/june-24-pr/the-idfs-use-of-data-technologies-in-intelligence-processing-published-june-18-2024/) use of Habsora- and Lavender-related data technologies while stating that the systems support human analysts and do not autonomously identify targets for attack. Reuters reported that the United States was examining allegations about AI-supported target identification, preserving their unverified status. AP later reported extensive use of commercial U.S. AI models and cloud services by the Israeli military. Together, these sources support the relevance of AI/data-assisted intelligence to the conflict while leaving autonomy, workflow, and authority allocation contested.

### 4. Humanitarian consequences provide an independent evidence channel

UNICEF documented grave harm to children, civilian infrastructure, and essential services from the opening days of the conflict. This evidence is relevant to harm assessment, proportionality review, continuity, and governance reassessment. It does not give UNICEF targeting or offensive-authorisation authority.

### 5. Neutral intermediaries perform a different function from threat detectors

The ICRC documented its role as a neutral intermediary facilitating agreed hostage releases and maintaining humanitarian dialogue with the parties. That role supports the governance importance of independent verification, negotiation, human conditions, and intermediary evidence as channels distinct from automated threat detection. The ICRC does not possess military targeting or offensive-authorisation authority.

### 6. Technology–military convergence is context, not mechanism evidence

The U.S. Army Reserve's description of Andrew Bosworth's appointment to an executive innovation detachment shows a broader institutional convergence between technology leadership and military advisory roles. It provides no evidence that Bosworth joined the Israeli military, entered Gaza, participated in the reviewed theatre, or contributed to any authority-renewal mechanism. It is therefore contextual background only.

## Counter-evidence and alternative explanations

The IDF's stated position is the principal counter-evidence to any claim that Habsora or Lavender autonomously selects targets: it says the tools assist analysts, do not autonomously identify targets for attack, and remain within human analytical and command processes. That statement does not resolve all external allegations, but it must remain alongside critical reporting because the autonomy question is contested.

Renewed fighting after a hostile event can be explained by human political and military decisions, pre-existing rules of engagement, treaty or truce interpretations, operational plans, intelligence assessments, or legal advice. Temporal sequence alone does not identify which explanation controlled a particular decision.

A defensive interception system and an offensive targeting system may be technically, organisationally, and legally separate. The governance abstraction does not imply a shared runtime, shared vendor, shared control plane, or shared chain of command.

Humanitarian reporting can reveal conditions and consequences without determining military legality, attribution, target validity, or operational authority. Neutral intermediary activity likewise shows the value of distinct human information channels without converting those intermediaries into decision authorities.

## Limitations

No reviewed source provides the complete technical architecture, targeting workflow, command authorisation record, rules of engagement, or machine logs needed to test the hypothesised coupling directly. Public statements and journalism may be incomplete, contested, or dependent on confidential sources. The review does not adjudicate the legality of particular operations or attribute responsibility for particular strikes.

The research did not identify a real-world incident in which a shared control disabled both an offensive AI system and an air-defence system. It also did not identify a deployed automated system that renewed offensive authority after detecting a hostile event. Those occurrences remain unverified.

## Source-level limitations and interpretive reliance

The AP Iron Dome explainer is relied on for the bounded interception description and reported saturation limits, not for governance architecture or offensive-system integration. The Reuters and AP truce-collapse reports are relied on for attributed statements and event sequence only; neither is interpreted as a command-authority trace. The IDF statement is both factual evidence of data-technology use and explicit counter-evidence on autonomous target identification, while its institutional position and lack of full workflow disclosure remain limitations. Reuters' AI-targeting report is relied on to establish that allegations were under examination, not that they were proven. The AP technology investigation supports commercial AI/cloud relevance but not automated authorisation. UNICEF is relied on for humanitarian conditions, and the ICRC for neutral-intermediary activity; neither is used to infer military targeting authority. The Army Reserve source is confined to contextual technology–military convergence and carries no interpretive weight concerning the reviewed theatre or mechanism.

## Governance implications

The evidence supports treating the following as distinct governance states:

1. detection of a threat;
2. bounded protective response;
3. verification of continuing conditions; and
4. authority for offensive force.

A threat signal can be valid and still be insufficient to confer offensive authority. An immediate defensive exception can be necessary and still be non-portable into retaliation, pursuit, or target selection. A later hostile act can require fresh assessment without reviving expired authority. Independent humanitarian and neutral-intermediary evidence should inform reassessment without being collapsed into a threat-detection or command-authority channel.

These are governance implications, not factual findings about any named deployment.

## Open questions

- Do any deployed systems share technical controls across defensive interception and offensive targeting, and under what accountable authority structure?
- What evidence and human roles are required to create, extend, expire, or revalidate offensive authority in actual command systems?
- How are immediate-defence exceptions encoded, logged, prevented from propagating, and tested?
- Can a system demonstrate that a new hostile act opened a new assessment rather than silently restoring prior authority?
- How can broad autonomy-suspension controls preserve independently authorised defensive continuity without creating a defensive exception that leaks into offence?
- What independent humanitarian, intermediary, legal, and ground-truth evidence is available to challenge threat-only recursive classification?
