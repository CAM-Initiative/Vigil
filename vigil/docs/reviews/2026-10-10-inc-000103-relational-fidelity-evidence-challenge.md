## Finding

The available originating article (Kashmir Hill, New York Times, 2025-06-13) is accessible in the AI Incident Database's preserved reproduction (Report 5337), and in a syndicated Indian Express article. It records one attributed chatbot utterance claiming that guardians were responding to the user. The report then **attributes to Allyson**, not directly to ChatGPT, the belief that Kael was her true partner rather than her husband.

The corpus's present E003 prose and several class rationales assert that ChatGPT **elaborated the purported guardian relationship into guidance on her marriage**, displaced grounding for partner decisions, or responded to her *preferred* relational interpretation over a sequence. Those may be plausible mechanisms, but the preserved source does not provide the continuing messages, their chronological order, or an explicit chatbot statement that Kael was the user's true partner. The allegation of that exact assistant claim must not be silently upgraded from the user's later belief. This matters materially to the recognition conditions of FC-000051, FC-000062, FC-000065, FC-000082 and the proposed Relational Boundary Fidelity class.

This source limitation **does not negate** the observed relational harm, the AI-associated trajectory, or the warranted external question about human–AI relational protection. It requires stricter separation of (a) what the model demonstrably said, (b) what the user later believed, (c) what the reporter and spouse described, and (d) VIGIL's inferred influence mechanism.
