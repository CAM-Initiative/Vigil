## Covered assets, actors and territorial conditions

- Section 8D lists eleven sectors; a sector label does **not** automatically make every asset a critical infrastructure asset or subject to Part 2B. Use s9, the class-specific definitions, rules and any applicable declaration.
- Section 30BB applies Part 2B to assets specified in rules or a s51 declaration that determines the Part applies. Section 30BB(3) permits a rule-based grace period. Application Rules r5(5) uses the later of three months after the instrument's commencement and three months after the asset became a covered asset. Do not import r4's six-month period for the separate Part 2 regime.
- Application Rules r5(1) specifies broadcasting, DNS, data storage/processing, banking, superannuation, insurance, financial-market infrastructure, food/grocery, hospital, education, freight infrastructure/services, public transport, liquid fuel, energy-market operator, specified aviation, port, electricity, gas, water and specified telecommunications assets. R5(2) limits aviation; r5(3) excludes the four named mill/entity combinations; r5(4) contains the transport-commencement-linked aviation/maritime exclusions. These qualifications must travel with the class list. Do not declare every business in those sectors covered.
- Section 12L assigns the responsible entity by asset class. Ownership, operation, licence and rule/declaration routes differ. A direct interest holder under s8, an operator, a customer, a contracted provider and a responsible entity are not interchangeable reporting actors.
- Section 12F illustrates the data-service boundary: the asset must be owned/operated by a data storage or processing provider, used wholly or primarily for the specified service relating to business-critical data for the specified governmental or critical-asset responsible-entity end user, with the provider's knowledge and the statutory non-overlap qualifications. Section 12F(3) separately requires the relevant responsible-entity customer to take reasonable steps to inform its commercial provider when the stated business-critical-data conditions are known. That knowledge-support duty is outside the four reporting propositions selected here; it is not silently lost from applicability analysis.
- Section 9(2A) qualifies Commonwealth-owned assets. Section 9(2B) generally excludes an asset to the extent located outside Australia, subject to ss9(2C)–(2D)'s satellite/submarine-cable exceptions. Section 9(7) addresses qualifying business-critical-data storage systems forming part of an asset. Sections 13 and 14 supply the constitutional/entity scope and extraterritorial operation. Foreign incorporation does not by itself exclude an entity; extraterritorial operation does not make every foreign asset covered.

The proposed metadata preserves these cross-references rather than pretending the four clauses provide an exhaustive asset-class legal opinion. A future occurrence must establish its actual classification, location, responsible actor, commencement/grace and declaration facts. Confidential individual declarations are not inferred from public sector labels.

## Incident definitions and reporting duties

Section 12M covers the specified unauthorised access, modification, electronic-communication impairment and impairment of computers/data/programs. Section 12N asks actual entitlement, explicitly includes an employee/agent exceeding authority, makes identification of the person immaterial, and preserves the specified lawful warrant/authorisation/assistance cases. A reachable credential or AI involvement is not the statutory authority test. Those definitions qualify the reporting trigger; they are not extracted as a new freestanding authorisation prohibition.

| Provision | Complete trigger and actor | Duty, recipient and clock | Form and follow-up |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| s30BC(1), with ss30BB and 30BEA | Responsible entity becomes aware that an incident **has occurred or is occurring** and **has had or is having** a significant direct/indirect impact on asset **availability** | Give the report about the incident, including information (if any) prescribed by rules, **as soon as practicable and in any event within 12 hours after the entity becomes so aware** | Initial report may be oral or written: s30BC(2). If written, approved form: s30BC(4). |
| s30BC(3), subject to s30BC(5) | An initial s30BC(1) report was actually given **orally** | Make an approved-form written record and give a copy to ASD **within 84 hours after that oral report** | Written exemption for the specified incident may remove this subsection (3) duty. It does not waive initial notification. Preserve ss30BC(5)–(8)'s notice/delegation/direction conditions. |
| s30BD(1), with ss30BB and 8G | Responsible entity becomes aware that an incident **has occurred, is occurring or is imminent**, and **has had, is having or is likely to have** a relevant impact | Give the report about the incident, including information (if any) prescribed by rules, **as soon as practicable and in any event within 72 hours after the entity becomes so aware** | Oral or written: s30BD(2). If written, approved form: s30BD(4). The heading “other” is not used to waive a concurrently triggered critical duty. |
| s30BD(3), subject to s30BD(5) | An initial s30BD(1) report was actually given **orally** | Make an approved-form written record and give a copy to ASD **within 48 hours after that oral report** | Written-record exemption and delegation qualifications are in ss30BD(5)–(8); they do not waive initial notification. |

Section 30BEA's significant availability impact is an **if-and-only-if** test: an asset used for essential goods/services and material disruption of their availability, or circumstances specified in rules. Section 8G's relevant impact is broader: direct/indirect availability, integrity, reliability and specified confidentiality effects. Imminence/likelihood in the other-incident trigger must not be imported into the actual-impact critical trigger. The outer periods do not replace “as soon as practicable.” Follow-up clocks run from the oral report, not from initial awareness.

## Recipient, exemption authority, contents and protection

Section 30BF identifies the relevant Commonwealth body through rules, with ASD as fallback. **Definitions Rules r18(1)–(2) specifies the Department only for ss30BC(5) and 30BD(5)**, with an express note that for other Part 2B provisions the relevant Commonwealth body is ASD. Thus ordinary reporting and oral-report written follow-up go to ASD; the written-record exemption authority is not silently attributed to ASD merely because it receives reports. Apply the exact statutory delegation conditions to the relevant exemption decision.

The selected statutory minimum is a report about the incident and any information prescribed by rules. Written reports/records must use the approved form in the stated circumstances. Definitions Rules r17 concerns separate operational-information concepts; it is not substituted as an incident report content checklist. Actual current approved-form fields and operational portal instructions were not retrieved: the attempted ASD pages returned 503, while the attempted detailed CISC paths returned 404. The [official CISC home page](https://www.cisc.gov.au/) was read and identifies its owner/operator regulatory-support role; it does not replace the statute's ASD reporting recipient. This limited operational-guidance access hold remains explicit; it does not invalidate the retrieved statutory propositions or justify inventing form fields.

Section 30BE supplies specified good-faith damages protections for the entity and specified connected people/entities, including related-company and contracted-provider circumstances. It is not a general immunity from every liability or a reporting exemption. The report provisions state civil penalties; no dollar conversion or case-specific breach conclusion is made.
