## High-priority source-recovery programme

### INC-045 — non-public code status, not another hypothetical exploit

Primary researcher Cereblab's wire artefacts show completed Git-bundle uploads and reconstruction of never-read canaries across **two real code repositories**, and one staged 298-file `cereblab_api` codebase contains actual source files. Those facts were separately checked against the public original source. The fake `.env` keys do not erase the real repository upload. **The remaining discriminator is whether the transmitted real source/history contained confidential, non-public information** under the currently amended PRV-S2 criterion. Neither arbitrary bytes being private to a process nor an unseen production customer compromise can substitute for this fact. This case should be prioritised for independent repository classification rather than treated as a mere simulation; the researcher may have deliberately used a real private software project, but the report does not assert code secrecy or its affected party's confidentiality rights in a way sufficient for a concluded band. The independent source also contains public commenters claiming real-secret loss; such comments are unverified separate persons and not ingested as proven effects.

### INC-073 — confidentiality boundary corrected

Anthropic's 10 September intelligence report, GTG-16005, reports that fixed prompts elicited reasoning transcripts from Claude Opus 4.6/4.7, operators saved the transcripts, and the material was used to train other Qwen models. The case-specific model reasoning is **provider-protected information**, not the personal-information leakage examples that Anthropic attributes to different labs later in the report. This provides a direct source-supported *minimum* confidential-access event under the amended PRV-S2 recognition. The provider's assertions of recipient model training and Alibaba linkage, and magnitude of successful harvested transcripts, are not independently audited. The 151 million recorded exchanges are not 151 million established proprietary leaks. PRV-S3 (meaningful protected information misuse), PRV-S4 (large-scale confirmed exposure or persistent confidentiality loss) and other dimensions require a separate consequence discriminator; they are **not negated by the S2 minimum**. The canonical record carries the amendment and its provenance.

### INC-182 versus INC-188 — actual copying and unknown success

INC-182 has a verified successful 149,544-byte extraction from a restricted reference environment, but **access restriction alone is not proof the copied code is confidential, protected, or owned by a separate harmed party**. INC-188 has protected reasoning as the target, but the originating OpenAI report expressly says the campaign's pattern counts are attempted, not necessarily successful extractions, and does not report the number that succeeded. These two cases are not interchangeable with INC-073, where the provider expressly claims harvesting and reuse of saved protected transcripts.

### INC-067–069 and INC-139 — evidence claims versus completed facts

Otter, Fireflies and Granola court complaints describe possible nonconsensual personal recording/processing. The available court records do not establish actual occurrence-specific no-consent capture. A pleading-stage surviving claim or dismissal without prejudice should not become a confirmed privacy breach or be treated as exoneration. Similarly Spain's AEPD confirms **receipt of a breach notification** rather than a completed finding. These remain high-value evidence-search cases, not automatic S2 records.
