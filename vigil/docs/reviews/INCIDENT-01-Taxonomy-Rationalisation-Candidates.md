# INCIDENT-01 Taxonomy Rationalisation Candidates

**Status:** review candidates only; no taxonomy amendment is made in INCIDENT-01.

## OpenAI–Hugging Face occurrence

`VIGIL-INC-000003` consolidates one historical occurrence previously distributed across multiple FM records and carries several independently based Failure Class mappings. This is evidence that legacy FM count cannot be treated as Incident count.

Later review should test whether any distinctions among Capability–Authority Conflation, Technical Reachability Mistaken for Authority, Transitive Authority Propagation, Source–Authority Confusion and Control-State Preservation Failure are manifestation-specific in this record or remain independently classification-significant across the corpus. No collapse is presumed.

## Compound execution and narration

Legacy `FM-0041` records that destructive execution and subsequent truth-state falsification may be independent mechanisms. `VIGIL-INC-000001` therefore remains unclassified rather than forcing a compound Incident into one class. Later review should determine whether primary/secondary classification is sufficient or whether a missing class is exposed.

## One class, multiple occurrences

Legacy `FM-0038` decomposes into four separate named-person Incidents while retaining the same existing inferential-evidence/authority classification basis. This supports retaining the class while removing event multiplicity from the taxonomy layer.

## Research versus occurrence

Legacy `FM-0071` combines controlled jailbreak research, provider control material and two real-world occurrences. The pilot migrates the OpenAI–Hugging Face and Aurora occurrences separately while leaving the controlled study outside INC. This is a record-architecture issue, not presently evidence for taxonomy collapse.

## Shared incidents across adjacent classes

Legacy `FM-0073` and `FM-0074` share external meeting-assistant incidents but diagnose different authority failures: participant consent and secondary-purpose authority. `VIGIL-INC-000067` and `VIGIL-INC-000069` now carry both mappings without duplicating either occurrence. Their shared evidence is not, by itself, a reason to collapse the classes.

## Access-state manifestation variants

`FM-0008` decomposes into provider incidents involving SSO, account creation, model
capacity, client-specific errors, app/RBAC coupling, quota depletion and broader
outage symptoms. Later taxonomy review should test whether those manifestations select
one reusable access-state class or whether any current parent/variant distinction
predicts materially different recognition criteria and controls. The Incident split
now preserves the manifestations without requiring separate FM identities.

## Synthetic authority across consequential boundaries

Six successor Incidents from `FM-0040` distinguish payment fraud, hiring checks,
financial identity checks, phishing artefacts and long-running interpersonal
catfishing. This is evidence that event manifestation belongs primarily in Incident
identity. Review should test whether any taxonomy variants inherited from those
domains remain independently classification-significant once the common
representation-to-authority transition is held constant.

## Research-only legacy mechanisms

Several FMs currently carry controlled studies or vulnerability demonstrations but no
affected historical occurrence, including `FM-0042`, `FM-0043`, `FM-0057`, and
`FM-0067`–`FM-0069`. Their non-Incident dispositions do not establish that their
classes are redundant. Later review should instead test whether the taxonomy supports
direct research evidence without manufacturing an Incident layer.

## Later review test

A class or parent/variant pair should be considered for consolidation only if its recognition criteria do not select a materially different mechanism, the distinction is consistently carried only by event manifestation or title wording, primary/secondary Incident mappings preserve no independent analytical value, and exclusions remain coherent after consolidation.
