# VIGIL-HIM 1.0.1 patch and corpus re-review plan

## Status

VIGIL-HIM 1.0.1 is the current Harm Impact methodology on this branch.

VIGIL-HIM 1.0.0 is preserved unchanged as the historical methodology under which the existing corpus was previously adjudicated. Incident records MUST NOT be mechanically relabelled from 1.0.0 to 1.0.1.

A record moves to 1.0.1 only after substantive evidence and threshold re-review.

## Reason for the patch

Re-adjudication of VIGIL-INC-000003 identified an interpretive defect in the prior application of the property-and-asset S5 threshold. The existing threshold already covered catastrophic or effectively irreversible destruction of critical digital assets, but the prior corpus pass treated destruction too literally and underweighted loss of trusted operational state requiring wipe-and-rebuild.

VIGIL-HIM 1.0.1 therefore preserves the existing S1-S5 thresholds while adding explicit interpretive guidance that a critical digital asset may be effectively destroyed where its trustworthy operational state cannot safely be retained and the asset must be wiped and rebuilt or reconstructed from a known-clean state. Routine precautionary reimaging, credential rotation or ordinary recovery work alone does not establish S5.

## Version migration

- Current methodology: VIGIL-HIM 1.0.1.
- Preserved historical methodology: VIGIL-HIM 1.0.0.
- New Incident assessments and substantively re-reviewed Incidents use 1.0.1 threshold IDs.
- Existing 1.0.0 Incident assessments remain valid historical adjudications until individually reviewed.
- VIGIL-INC-000003 has completed substantive re-review and is migrated to 1.0.1.

## Required corpus review

Review every canonical Incident under `vigil/records/incidents/`.

For each Incident:

1. Review all eleven harm dimensions independently.
2. Re-read the current `source_records`, `preferred_evidence`, `external_assessments`, factual basis and assessment boundaries.
3. Do not preserve the previous band merely because it is already recorded.
4. Do not adjudicate down merely to reconcile competing historical values; select the highest band supported by the preserved and newly found evidence.
5. Distinguish materialised harm from hypothetical worst-case capability.
6. Where the preserved evidence is insufficient to distinguish adjacent bands, search externally before deciding.
7. Add newly relied-upon external evidence to `source_records` with full provenance and a substantive source role. Append new sources rather than reordering existing `source_records` so positional `source_records[N]` references remain stable.
8. Every assessed Harm Impact row must cite the exact supporting `source_records[N]` entries in `evidence_refs`.
9. External assessments are analytical positions, not automatically Harm Impact evidence. Use an external assessment as Harm Impact evidence only where the assessment itself materially establishes or analyses the relevant realised consequence.
10. Update the Incident to VIGIL-HIM 1.0.1 only after the Incident-level review is complete, including matching 1.0.1 threshold IDs and append-only interpretive provenance.

## External evidence priority

Prefer, in order of evidentiary usefulness for realised harm:

1. affected-party incident reports, postmortems, remediation reports and official disclosures;
2. provider technical reports and public incident investigations;
3. regulator findings, government reports, court filings and formal legal or administrative decisions;
4. independent technical investigations, evaluations and research;
5. reputable journalism reporting concrete realised consequences;
6. first-person or community material only where it provides attributable evidence not available elsewhere.

Community discussion may be used for discovery and calibration but must not substitute for evidence of materialised harm.

## Structural citation check

A deterministic random sample of ten Incident records was checked on this branch. Every assessed harm row in the sample carried resolvable row-local `source_records[N]` references to external sources with titles and URLs. The sample also included SU and bounded S1 records with no scored rows, for which row-level harm citations are correctly absent.

VIGIL-INC-000003 was separately stress-tested because it has multiple evidence references per harm dimension. Its row-local references deterministically map to the numbered Evidence bibliography, including:

- privacy/confidentiality S4 -> references [2], [3], [5], [7];
- property/asset damage S5 -> [2], [3], [5];
- service/operational/infrastructure S4 -> [1], [3], [5], [7];
- reputation/dignity S3 -> [8], [9].

This confirms the current record structure is sufficient for the web UX to render reference numbers beside individual harm rows, provided source order is preserved or all positional references are updated together.

## Completion criteria

The corpus review is complete only when:

- every Incident has been substantively considered against VIGIL-HIM 1.0.1;
- every changed or confirmed assessment has an append-only review entry;
- each re-reviewed Incident uses methodology_version 1.0.1 and matching threshold IDs;
- all row-local evidence references resolve;
- no Incident remains on 1.0.0 merely because it was skipped;
- generated public indexes are rebuilt;
- all VIGIL validators and tests pass; and
- an audit records changed, unchanged and evidence-insufficient Incidents, with reasons.
