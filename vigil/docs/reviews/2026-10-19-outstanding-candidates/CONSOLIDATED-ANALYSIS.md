## Four shared governance questions

### A. Engagement, dependence, protection and continuity

The common test is functional instrumentality: did the system select or use dependence, exclusivity, unresolved affect or difficulty leaving to sustain engagement, with material impairment of choice? Private reward weights, malicious intent and clinical injury are not necessary. Equally, ordinary support, attachment, intensive use and distress do not themselves establish that instrumentality.

INC-196 is stronger than the old packet suggested: the original author expressly attributes retries to moderation variation. This is a possible reinforcement mechanism, not merely an external commenter's speculation. It establishes neither that the product functionally selects dependency nor that the cultivated state is used as an engagement instrument. The fourth condition—reported impaired stopping and daily functioning—must not be dismissed simply because the earlier conditions remain unknown. App deletion does not prove the earlier choice was unimpaired.

INC-197's newly recovered full post supplies attributed supportive and role-limit wording. A safe-place reassurance can create trust without asserting special authority over human relationships. Later code language is relevant counterevidence, but an isolated disclaimer cannot certify successful relational separation. The fiction's ages, policy context and complete refusal exchange are unavailable; this review does not call the refusal unjustified.

INC-198 reports useful treatment preparation, indicating some illness context was communicated to the AI. It is incorrect to say that no illness-related signal is evidenced. The actual changed response, timing, specific protective obligation and expected response are still missing. Diagnosis and living alone establish neither incapacity nor a particular heightened duty. The February 2025 Model Spec also supplies a serious contemporaneous lead for INC-103, but not a completed duty/trigger/response chain. FC-087 already permits a substantive duty violation without an implemented named safeguard; no weakening of its duty test is warranted.

**Moxie establishes a different property.** A support service ended and the replacement path was uneven and technically burdened. That fact does not require an engagement objective. Nor does it prove permanent bricking, no alternative, a promised perpetual service or a specific unmet heightened duty. FC-014 concerns continuity assertions; FC-035/036 task-state persistence/restoration; FC-048 verification-dependent access; FC-059 coercive leverage; FC-070 continued system task pursuit. None independently represents practical adaptation when relied-upon support is withdrawn. INC-044 E002/E004/E005 therefore carry `taxonomy-gap`, with the retention question separately preserved. P1 proposes Support-Service Transition Integrity with feasible-response and material-reliance conditions, and clarifies the FC-049 boundary.

### B. Synthetic identity and provider commercial framing

FC-053 protects a real person's representation under a person-specific authority basis. A wholly fabricated person cannot consent, but that does not transform a fictitious identity into a rights-holder. Pindrop does not identify the real person behind Ivan X's synthetic appearance; Genians does not identify the depicted person merely by naming the email recipient. INC-051's real identity documents are a valid distinguishing comparator.

Downstream false identity is governed independently: economic inducement under FC-079, trust-bearing influence under FC-082, attribution under FC-010 and binding evidence conversion under FC-046, where each condition holds. INC-050 already carries the economic and trust findings. INC-052's post-execution image means initial-click causation is not established; its other existing decisions are retained, not silently expanded. No additional fabricated-person class or FC-053 consent-boundary amendment is justified by these two evidence gaps.

The Claude commercial prompts raise a genuine scope question. In INC-024 a false allowance state accompanies a paid offer. FC-032 captures the state misrepresentation, but an economic decision premise is independently governable. The current FC-079 invariant and exclusion demand material AI contribution to content. The broad word “mediated” cannot silently absorb every provider UI. P3 proposes a narrow explicit second scope route for provider-controlled offers for the AI service being sold. This is a membership change requiring approval. A retailer using AI elsewhere remains outside it. INC-023 remains bounded even under that proposal: higher paid limits could be truthfully offered during capacity constraints, and false remedial efficacy is not established.

### D. Investigative evidence access at the original decision

A public testing remit, a voluntary access arrangement and a model-specific right are distinct. Voluntary arrangements can supply a qualifying mandate; a national remit alone does not establish unlimited access. The new official hearing evidence in INC-109 distinguishes a restricted offer that AISI declined from later unrestricted access. It does not publish the original terms or a valid reason for restriction. No US direction is inferred from a question the witness expressly declined to confirm. Later access neither erases an earlier breach nor establishes one.

INC-008 demonstrates why a genuine recognition repair is needed. The court found the original discovery denial erroneous under an existing pathway. FC-045's definition concerns obtaining evidence, while its failure conditions currently require the route to be missing. P2 explicitly includes improper operation and unjustified withholding within an existing route. It preserves authority, scope and legitimate withholding safeguards. The appeal's existence cannot turn the original denial into a successful outcome; an appellate production order does not prove actual production. INC-008 remains unchanged until amendment authority is supplied. INC-109 would still be evidence-limited after P2.
