## Methodology review

VIGIL-HIM 1.0.1 retains the registered S1–S5 thresholds and the highest-supported-materialised-harm derivation rule. The methodology guidance was clarified before corpus migration to make the following applications explicit:

- cyber and digital harms must be separated across privacy, asset, service and financial dimensions rather than collapsed into one severity impression;
- restoration means return to a trustworthy pre-Incident operational state, including material backlogs and degraded modes;
- wipe-and-rebuild or known-clean reconstruction may evidence effective destruction of a critical digital asset, while routine reimaging or credential rotation does not by itself establish S5;
- adverse publicity is reputational exposure, not automatically reputational injury;
- legal and regulatory consequences are assessed through the material consequence they establish, not as an automatic severity escalator; and
- unresolved adjacent-band uncertainty is preserved as insufficient evidence rather than automatically resolved downward.

The accompanying publication standard was aligned to version 1.0.1 and a literal escaped-newline defect was removed.
