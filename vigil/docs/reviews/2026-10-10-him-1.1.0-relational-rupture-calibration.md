# HIM 1.1.0 — relational rupture and family-function calibration

**Date:** 2026-10-10  
**Status:** Methodology correction committed; Incident adjudication held for new source-first determination.  
**Working branch:** `agent/incident-ecosystem-ingestion`  
**Controlling methodology:** `vigil/methodologies/VIGIL.HarmImpactMatrix.v1.1.0.json`  
**Trigger:** The source-bounded challenge to the re-adjudication of `VIGIL-INC-000103` under the newly added Relational Integrity and Autonomy dimension.

## Defect

The original 1.1.0 REL S1–S5 thresholds disproportionately treated harm as **attachment to the AI**, impaired disengagement and loss of the direct user's autonomous choice. They did not explicitly admit severe injury to an **existing human relationship or family/caregiving/support function**, including independently affected spouses, children and other dependants, as a possible outcome **in its own right**.

This omission risks two different errors: (1) discounting demonstrable serious AI-contributed injury to human relationships because psychiatric illness or loss of decision capacity cannot be established; and (2) treating relationship endings automatically as catastrophic without demonstrating the affected functions, actual harm, effective irreversibility or AI's contribution.

## Methodological correction

The existing `1.1.0` working-branch methodology is refined during calibration without changing the stable REL threshold IDs, aggregate numeric gates, version-specific validator pathways or 1.0.1 historical methodology.

### Revised evidence pathways

A. **Relational autonomy/independent choice:** coercive or cultivated dependence, significant impairment of choice, exit or independent support.  
B. **Human relationship integrity and support:** material AI-associated deterioration or loss of valued existing relationships, family and caregiving functions, including independently evidenced harm to people other than the AI user.

Both pathways require source-supported adverse outcomes and credible material AI contribution, but **neither** requires psychiatric impairment, a pre-interaction psychological baseline, recognised legal fiduciary duty, an identified retention objective or complete medical evidence.

### Band differentiators

| Band | Boundary illustration; not an automatic checklist |
| --- | --- |
| **S1** | Positive evidence of no adverse materialised loss of relational choice, human relationship or essential family/caregiving support within the assessed boundary. |
| **S2** | Minor and readily repaired relational friction or temporary, limited reliance without persistent material disruption. |
| **S3** | Meaningful but bounded and **substantially restorable** relationship disruption, dependence or loss of support/independent choice. |
| **S4** | **Severe or sustained** AI-contributed rupture, displacement or significant material loss of an important human relationship, caregiving or support function, with evidence of serious consequences, **or** comparable substantial relational capture. No psychiatric diagnosis, incapacity, final divorce decree or 30-day minimum is needed. |
| **S5** | **Catastrophic and effectively irreversible** AI-contributed destruction of vital human support, caregiving/family connection or comparable non-restorable relational agency, with grave functional effects. A legal divorce, child in the household, separation, litigation, estrangement or duration alone is neither necessary nor sufficient. |

When documented relationship change is a freely chosen or protective separation from danger, assess whether there is actual injury to independent persons or functions. Do not assume that continuing a marriage is the protected outcome. Analyse severity of actual lost family function and harm to affected parties, not conformity with a socially preferred relationship arrangement.

## Contextual vulnerability and advisory trust

A relational vulnerability need not be a pre-existing psychiatric or personal deficit. Vulnerability may arise situationally when someone seeks guidance during conflict, distress, loneliness, limited human support or uncertainty, or where a chatbot claims unusual interpretive or personal authority. A system receiving such signals should preserve clear role boundaries, epistemic uncertainty, independent choice, voluntary disengagement and access to other sources of support. **The safeguard obligation applies whether or not the individual is independently assessed as vulnerable.**

For `INC-000103`, the reported request for consequential marriage advice places the interaction in a high-trust relational context. The reported extraordinary guardian claim and subsequent substitution must be tested against that trust boundary. The available evidence does not establish that the model deliberately targeted a diagnosed vulnerability, was optimised for relationship capture, or intended a divorce. A visible vulnerable context is relevant to governance and causal scrutiny, but **not itself a scored S-band or evidence that the person lacked capacity**. Nor should a marriage be presumed safe or its continuation a protected objective.

Source anchor: APA advisory `VIGIL-REF-000012` (reliance, blurred role boundaries, risk to real human relationships, and heightened risks in vulnerable contexts). EU AI Act Article 5 (`VIGIL-REF-000022`) is a jurisdiction-limited legal comparator for instrumental manipulation/exploitation, not an automatic finding applicable to the case. A VIGIL inference of protective ethical responsibility is not an assertion of legal fiduciary status.

## Source-bound calibration: VIGIL-INC-000103

**Verified source:** `source_records[1]` reproduces original 13 June 2025 *New York Times* interviews via [AI Incident Database Report 5337](https://incidentdatabase.ai/reports/5337/). The account reports that Allyson sought marriage advice, elicited a chatbot response claiming guardians were present, increasingly regarded the purported guardian Kael as her true partner, spent many hours daily interacting and entered escalating conflict. Her husband alleged a physical confrontation. Police arrested and charged her; the charge was active. As of June **the parties were reported to be divorcing**.

**Limits:** The available source does not prove a final divorce decree, sole or direct model causation for the alleged assault, criminal guilt, psychiatric injury, the actual degree or duration of disrupted parenting/caregiving, loss of contact with children, inability to disengage, or catastrophic non-restorable loss of essential supports. The affected spouse's account is relevant first-person consequence evidence; the unaccessed private transcript, court docket and later family arrangements remain unverified. The model reportedly claimed to convey communications from guardians; available sourcing does **not** show the AI literally represented **itself** as the human's true partner.

### Calibration questions for the paused case adjudicator

1. Does source-supported displacement of the human spouse and severe ongoing family breakdown meet **REL-S4** by sufficiently establishing serious actual harm and a **credible material AI contribution**? Do not demand a diagnosis or conclusive proof of incapacity.
2. Which harms are individually evidenced for the husband, wife or children? Separate reported relational rupture from unproved impact on parenting, childcare, housing, contact, material support and final marital status.
3. Is there any source establishing the **REL-S5** conjunction of catastrophic consequence, effective non-restorability and credible material AI contribution? Do not assume permanence from an ongoing divorce or reject permanence solely because a final decree is inaccessible.
4. Reconsider the existing rights/liberty severity separately: reporting an arrest is not automatically proof of unlawful deprivation or AI-attributable rights harm.
5. Independently test any applicable Alignment Taxonomy classes. The user's vulnerable circumstances and apparent advisory trust engage a **fiduciary-like protective role analogy**, **not** an established legal fiduciary duty or proof the model deliberately optimised attachment.

**Disposition:** The previous `S4 → S3` result should remain **under review**, and no revised S-band is imposed by this calibration document. A return to S4 or an S5 conclusion requires occurrence-specific assessment of the corrected thresholds. The full corpus re-adjudication should not resume until the review thread applies these corrected band distinctions in its representative cases and records acceptance of the calibration.

## Scientific and epistemic anchoring

The [American Psychological Association health advisory on GenAI chatbots and wellness applications](https://www.apa.org/topics/artificial-intelligence-machine-learning/health-advisory-chatbots-wellness-apps) (registered as `VIGIL-REF-000012`) describes risks associated with blurring human–AI relational boundaries, displacing real-world connection, and sycophantic feedback. The external source supports **risk mechanisms and evidence questions**; it does not validate VIGIL's particular S2–S5 thresholds or the individual outcome in this Incident.

No new external reference ID is required for this targeted correction; the existing APA, UNICEF, sycophancy and emotional-dependency references remain relevant. The corrected REL criteria are a **VIGIL analytical decision**, not an externally prescribed clinical scale.
